Dave's Hot Chicken DMCA: Takedown Policy

A copyright holder who finds their work on this guide can ask for it to be taken down, and the Dave's Hot Chicken DMCA page sets out exactly how that works — what a notice must contain, what happens after one arrives, and how a counter-notice is handled.

What the Dave's Hot Chicken DMCA Policy Covers

Copyright complaints about material on this guide go through the Dave's Hot Chicken DMCA process: a written notice, a review, and removal where the complaint checks out.

The policy covers text, images and other material published on the guide; it does not cover the restaurant chain, its own website, or its social media accounts, which are separate from this guide. The guide itself is the Dave's Hot Chicken menu, with prices, calories, hours, store listings and the guides that go with them.

The guide's own text, images and tool are original work written and built for the guide. Trademarks such as Dave's Hot Chicken belong to their owners, and the guide is independent, not affiliated with the chain. A notice can come from the copyright owner or from a person authorised to act for the owner, and the review starts from the notice itself, not from who sent it.

How to Send a Dave's Hot Chicken DMCA Takedown Notice

Notices go through the Dave's Hot Chicken contact page, which is the only route for takedown notices.

A notice sent anywhere else — a social media message, a comment, a store — is not a notice under this policy and is not acted on. A valid notice contains six elements:

ElementWhat to include
The copyrighted workIdentification of the work claimed to be infringed — a title, a link, or a description precise enough to find it.
The material on the guideThe URL of the material on this guide claimed to be infringing, with enough detail to locate it.
Contact detailsThe sender's name, email address and, where the sender has one, a postal address.
Good-faith statementA statement that the sender believes in good faith the use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
Accuracy statementA statement, made under penalty of perjury, that the information in the notice is accurate and that the sender is authorised to act for the copyright owner.
SignatureA physical or electronic signature of the sender or of the person authorised to act for the copyright owner.

A notice that leaves out one of the six elements is not complete. An incomplete Dave's Hot Chicken DMCA notice is returned through the contact route with the missing element named, and the material stays up until a complete notice arrives.

A trademark complaint, a price correction, or a question about the guide is not a copyright complaint, and the DMCA route is not the route for it. Trademark questions and corrections go through the contact page, and the Dave's Hot Chicken terms of service page sets out the rest of the rules for using the guide.

What Happens After a Dave's Hot Chicken DMCA Notice Arrives

Each complete notice is reviewed against the material it names, and the sender is told the outcome through the contact route the notice came in on.

Where the complaint checks out, the material is removed from the guide. Removal covers only the material the notice names. The rest of the guide is untouched, and a removal does not change any other page, image or tool. The sender is told what was removed and why, and the material stays down unless a valid counter-notice changes that.

Removal is not a finding about every use of the work, and it is not a judgement on the sender's claim beyond the material named. It is the guide's response to a complete notice, and it is the end of the takedown step unless a counter-notice arrives.

Counter-Notices

A person who believes material was removed by mistake, or that the use was authorised, can send a counter-notice through the same contact route.

A counter-notice must identify the removed material and where it appeared, state under penalty of perjury that the removal was a mistake or a misidentification, and give the sender's name and contact details.

A valid counter-notice is reviewed the same way as a takedown notice. Where the counter-notice checks out, the material is restored, and the original sender is told through the contact route. The counter-notice process runs on the same basis as the takedown process: no fixed number of days is promised, and each case is handled in the order it arrives.

Repeat Infringers

Material removed under a valid notice that is posted again is removed again, and repeated postings of the same infringing material are refused.

The policy treats a pattern of reposting as grounds to refuse further material from the same source. The guide takes no submissions from readers, so reposting means material returning to the guide through its own publishing. Where the same material returns after removal, it is taken down again without a fresh review of the original complaint.

Original Content and Trademarks

The text, images and tool on this guide are original work, and trademarks, names and branding of Dave's Hot Chicken belong to the restaurant chain.

A complaint about a trademark is not a copyright complaint, and it is not handled under the DMCA route. Copyright complaints use the route on this page. Anything else — a question about the guide, a correction to a price or an hour — goes through the Dave's Hot Chicken contact page, and the rest of the rules for using the guide sit on the Dave's Hot Chicken terms of service page.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I send a Dave's Hot Chicken DMCA takedown notice?

Send a Dave's Hot Chicken DMCA notice through the contact page, which is the only route for takedown notices. The notice must contain all six elements listed in the takedown section: identification of the work, the URL of the material, contact details, a good-faith statement, an accuracy statement under penalty of perjury, and a signature. An incomplete notice is returned with the missing element named.

What must a takedown notice contain?

Six things: identification of the copyrighted work, the URL of the material on the guide, the sender's contact details, a good-faith statement, a statement under penalty of perjury that the information is accurate, and a physical or electronic signature. A Dave's Hot Chicken DMCA notice missing any of the six is returned as incomplete.

What happens after a takedown notice is sent?

The notice is reviewed against the material it names. Where the complaint checks out, the material is removed and the sender is told the outcome through the contact route. A complete Dave's Hot Chicken DMCA notice is acted on; an incomplete one is returned with the missing element named.

Can I send a counter-notice if my material was removed by mistake?

Yes. Send a counter-notice through the same contact route, identifying the removed material, stating under penalty of perjury that the removal was a mistake or a misidentification, and giving contact details. Where the counter-notice checks out, the material is restored.
