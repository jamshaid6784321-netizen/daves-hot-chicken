Dave's Hot Chicken Combos: Every Meal Compared

Dave's Hot Chicken combos are five numbered meals, and the whole system fits in one sentence: Dave's #1 is two tenders, #2 is two sliders, #3 is one of each, #4 is a single slider and #5 is a single tender, every one of them with fries and Dave's Sauce. Dave's #2 is the most-ordered combo on the menu and #4 the cheapest build. This page compares all five Dave's Hot Chicken combos side by side with their official chart calories and protein, explains the two numbers that do not officially exist (#6 and #7), and covers the newer meals Dave's has added around the numbered five.

The Lineup: Dave's #1 to #5

How a combo is built matters as much as the count. Tenders arrive with sliced bread, pickles and Dave's Sauce; sliders come on a potato bun with kale slaw, pickles and Dave's Sauce; and the fries are crinkle-cut, seasoned, and in the tray by default. In all Dave's Hot Chicken combos, heat is set per piece, not per order, so the two halves of a Dave's #3 can run at different levels, and all seven spice levels are available in every combo at no extra charge. The Reaper level is the one exception to casual ordering: it needs a signed waiver first.

Which Combo Fits You?

Five Dave's Hot Chicken combos, five different appetites. Matched by what each meal actually does best:

Dave's #5: the light full meal

One tender and fries at 930 to 990 calories. The smallest numbered combo and the lightest chicken meal on the chart; the right size for a normal lunch.

Dave's #4: the cheapest way in

One slider and fries, 1,060 to 1,110 calories. In the listings checked for our homepage guide it was the cheapest combo on the menu, at $10.49 to $11.89 depending on the store.

Dave's #3: a bit of both

A tender and a slider together, 1,370 to 1,490 calories and 62g of protein. The first-timer's combo, because it settles the tender-versus-slider argument in one tray.

Dave's #1: the classic

Two tenders and fries, 1,230 to 1,350 calories, 56g of protein. The purest form of the menu: no bun, no build, just the chicken the chain is named for.

Dave's #2: the big appetite

Two sliders and fries, 1,500 to 1,620 calories and the most protein of any combo at 68g. The most-ordered combo at Dave's, and the one that ends the meal for most people.

Sharing, or two people?

A combo is built for one. For two people, two single combos (#4 or #5) beat splitting a #2, and for a table, the Hot Boxes in the last section are the actual group format.

The #5 Mystery, and the Missing #6 and #7

Dave's #5 is real and documented, but you may not find it on your store's menu. And the Dave's Hot Chicken combos numbering genuinely stops at five.

Dave's #5, one tender with fries, appears in Dave's official nutrition and allergen documentation with full figures (930 to 990 calories, 31g of protein), yet it is missing from the live ordering menus of many stores, where the numbered line effectively runs #1 to #4. The likeliest reading is that #5 survives in the chain's documents while individual menus trim it. If your store lists it, it is the lightest combo Dave's makes; if it does not, ordering a single tender with a side of fries rebuilds it exactly.

As for a Dave's combo 6 or combo 7: neither exists in the official lineup, in the nutrition documents or on ordering menus. Searches for them surface because the numbering invites the guess, and because other chains run longer combo lists. At Dave's, five is the whole set, and anything labelled #6 or #7 on a fan page is an error.

Combos Are "Dave's Meals", and the Line Is Growing

On Dave's own menus, Dave's Hot Chicken combos sit under the name Dave's Meals, and the meals section now holds more than the numbered five. The combo is simply the meal format: a main, fries and Dave's Sauce, priced as one item instead of three.

Big Trio

Your choice of three from tenders, Spicy Buffalo Mac & Cheese, Hot Mozz, Dave's Bites or Mini Sliders, with three sauces. A build-your-own meal that sits outside the numbering entirely.

Mac Loaded Slider

The slider that began as a fan creation in a Dave's parking lot and was made official: a slider loaded with mac & cheese, also sold with fries as a meal.

Mini Sliders

Four mini sliders, with a fries version as a meal. A shorter heat list than the big items, running Not Hot, Medium and Hot on the menus that list them.

These newer Dave's Hot Chicken combos have no rows in the published nutrition chart yet, which is why the comparison table above stays with the numbered five: their calories are not guessed here. The nutrition page tracks what the chart does publish, meal by meal.

Combo Prices: Why There Is No National List

Dave's publishes no national combo prices, because there is nothing national to publish: the restaurants are franchised and each store sets its own prices, which is why Dave's own menu page routes you to a location finder instead of a price list.

Three practical consequences follow. First, any single price printed for a Dave's combo, on any site, describes one store at one moment; treat ranges as the honest format. Second, delivery runs above counter prices: menu trackers that compare both put delivery listings roughly 20 to 25 percent higher than ordering direct, so the cheapest version of any of the Dave's Hot Chicken combos is the one you pick up yourself. Third, there are no standing combo deals to hunt; the value end of the line is structural, the #4 and #5, and the savings that do exist live in the app and the rewards program. To price your own order exactly, your store's page in the official ordering system is the only source that can.

Feeding More People: From Combos to Hot Boxes

Dave's Hot Chicken combos are built for one person, and a family-size numbered meal does not exist, because the group format at Dave's is a different product: the Hot Box.

Hot Boxes scale the same pieces the combos are built from: 10 tenders, 10 sliders, 40 pc Bites, a 20-piece box of Mini Sliders, a Hot Mozz box, cauliflower boxes and a box of fries, plus the Hot Box Roulette, which mixes 4 Mild, 5 Medium and 1 Reaper piece in one box of ten. For an actual event rather than a big family dinner, the same boxes anchor Dave's catering, which the catering guide prices and explains. The line this page draws is simple: one person, order a combo; a table, order a box.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between Dave's #1 through #5?

The main piece and its count. #1 is two tenders, #2 is two sliders, #3 is one tender and one slider, #4 is one slider and #5 is one tender. Every one of the Dave's Hot Chicken combos includes fries and Dave's Sauce, and heat is set per piece in all of them.

What is the most popular Dave's Hot Chicken combo?

Dave's #2, the two-slider combo, is the most-ordered meal on the menu by multiple accounts. It is also the biggest: 1,500 to 1,620 calories and 68g of protein.

What is the cheapest Dave's combo?

Dave's #4, one slider with fries. It is the smallest slider build, and in the listings checked for our homepage guide it ran $10.49 to $11.89 depending on the store. Exact prices are set per restaurant, so check your store's menu for the current figure.

How many calories are in a Dave's #2 combo?

1,500 to 1,620 calories for the two sliders and fries, the range being the spice bands from Not Hot to Reaper. It also carries 68g of protein, 143 to 145g of carbs and the highest sodium of the numbered combos at the top of its range.

Can you mix spice levels inside one Dave's combo?

Yes. Heat is set per piece, not per order, so a Dave's #3 can run one piece Mild and the other Extra Hot at no extra charge. The only level with a condition is Reaper, which needs a signed waiver.

Is there a Dave's Hot Chicken combo #6 or #7?

No. The numbered lineup stops at #5, and #5 itself is documented in Dave's official nutrition files but missing from many stores' ordering menus. Anything sold as a Dave's #6 or #7 is not part of the official Dave's Hot Chicken combos.

Does Dave's have family meal deals?

Not as numbered combos. The group format is the Hot Box range: boxes of 10 tenders or sliders, 40 pc Bites, minis and cauliflower boxes, built for sharing. For parties and offices, those same boxes anchor the catering menu.
