Dave's Hot Chicken Fallout Meal: The Survival Kit, Honestly

The Dave's Hot Chicken Fallout meal was $12.99 of vault-hunting fun — and it sold out fast. A limited-time collaboration with the Fallout television series, the Dave's x Fallout Saucy Survival Kits launched on January 7, 2026 at about 400 participating U.S. locations, while supplies lasted, and supplies did not last. What remains is a genuinely well-documented little promotion, a collectible people still hunt, and a meal you can almost entirely rebuild from today's regular menu. What the Collab Was

The Dave's Hot Chicken Fallout meal was a packaging-and-collectible promotion built on regular menu food: two existing Dave's meals, dressed in Fallout packaging, with a working Stimpak toy in the box.

Fallout Season 2 had premiered on December 16, 2025, and Dave's moved fast: the collab was announced on the brand's Instagram on its release date, January 7, 2026. The Dave's Hot Chicken Fallout meal menu held no new food at all, which was the honest charm of it: the kitchen changed nothing, the box changed everything. One clarification: it was not a spice challenge. The kits came at any of Dave's seven spice levels at no extra cost, so a Fallout meal could be Not Hot. The wasteland, it turns out, caters to all tolerances.

Yes, the slider kit was the #4 in a costume: one slider with fries is exactly the #4 combo configuration from the regular menu, at combo-plus-collectible money. The Bites kit was the better feed; the slider kit had the better box art. Collectors, predictably, wanted both.

The Stimpak: a Collectible That Works

In the games, a Stimpak is the healing injector the player character jabs for instant health. Dave's version is a replica that actually does its job in the real world: it is a sauce dispenser.

The design, as filmed and photographed after launch: gray body, orange stripe, a meter detail reading up to 300, sized like the in-game item and bagged inside the kit box. Fill it with ranch or Dave's Sauce, and the plunger injects the sauce onto the food, which is why every review kept demonstrating it on camera; it is a toy you use during the meal, not a statue you shelve after. For collectors buying now, the checks that matter: the Stimpak should be the gray-and-orange dispenser with the meter detail, a complete kit means the Stimpak plus its box packaging, and a loose dispenser without the box is the common, cheaper find. No "what it is worth" figure is printed as fact: resale listings sit around $25 to $30 on a single source, so treat that as the shape of the market, not a valuation.

Is It Still Available?

No. Shoppers still ask when does it end, and there was never an end date to miss: the promotion ran while supplies lasted at each location, so stores sold out on their own clocks, busy ones in days. The end date question, and its twin, how long did it run, have the same honest answer: until your store's Stimpaks ran out, and Dave's announced no restock.

The shape of the launch geography: no participating-store list was ever published, which is why a near me search or a locations search never had an answer, then or now. The promotion ran at roughly 400 U.S. locations, and the searched-for places read like a Dave's map: Dallas, Chicago, Los Angeles, Houston, Atlanta, Miami, New York City (NYC) and Brooklyn, San Diego, Phoenix, Austin, Denver, Seattle, Portland, Sacramento, San Antonio, Las Vegas, Orlando, Nashville, Charlotte and Columbus among them. Whether any single store on that list participated was a local fact, and by the time most people searched, the local answer was gone. Reddit tracked exactly that, sell-out reports store by store; the price questions there are the resale era arriving.

Internationally: the Dave's Hot Chicken Fallout meal uk launch was trailed in the announcement and the Middle East channels teased the collab, with dates varying by market; a canada rollout came with less confirmation, and a london or toronto search today lands on the same sold-out story as the U.S. one. No specific international dates are printed, because none stand at the standard of the U.S. launch facts above.

Recreate the Fallout Meal Today

The food never left. Because both kits were regular menu builds, the Dave's Hot Chicken Fallout meal on your table tonight is an order, not a hunt, minus the box and the toy.

The Bites kit, rebuilt

Order the 10 pc Saucy Bites (Buffalo, Spicy BBQ or Hot Honey) with fries and a ranch dipper. That is the kit, item for item: saucy bites, crispy fries, ranch. Pick your heat; the original let you, too.

The Slider kit, rebuilt

Order combo #4, one slider with fries, and add the ranch. The #4 was the slider kit before the costume and remains it after; the combos guide has its full spec.

What cannot be rebuilt

The Fallout packaging and the Stimpak. Those were the promotion. If the box matters to you, it is a resale purchase now, and the Stimpak section above lists what a complete kit should include.

On nutrition, the kits inherit the regular menu's numbers, and the Dave's Hot Chicken Fallout meal calories work out from the chart like this, as arithmetic rather than official kit figures: the slider kit is the #4 combo at 1,060 to 1,110 calories plus the ranch dipper at 200, so roughly 1,260 to 1,310. The Bites kit runs the Saucy Bites (670 in Buffalo, 810 in Spicy BBQ, 920 in Hot Honey) plus fries at 440 and the ranch at 200, so roughly 1,310 to 1,560 depending on the sauce. The calories threads on reddit land in the same range by the same method. Full figures live in the nutrition guide; the nutrition story has no separate chart, because there was no separate food. Cross-answers, briefly: the Dave's Hot Chicken Fallout meal halal answer is the chain's (the kits were standard chicken; see the halal guide), the allergen menu and gluten free answers are the standard menu's cautious ones (see those guides), and the secret menu connection is that there is none: it was an official limited-time item, the opposite of a secret one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Dave's Hot Chicken Fallout meal?

A limited-time collaboration between Dave's Hot Chicken and the Fallout TV series: two 'Saucy Survival Kits' sold from January 7, 2026. Each kit was a regular Dave's meal (10 pc Saucy Bites, or a single slider in the #4 combo build) with fries and ranch, in Fallout-themed packaging, with a working Stimpak sauce-dispenser toy in the box. Both kits were $12.99 at about 400 participating U.S. locations, while supplies lasted.

How much was the Dave's Hot Chicken Fallout meal?

$12.99 for both kits. No national price list is published, so local variation was possible, but $12.99 is the documented launch price and the one to measure resale bundles against.

What came in the Fallout Survival Kit?

The Bites kit: 10 pc Saucy Bites, crispy fries, ranch, Fallout packaging and the Stimpak dispenser. The w/ Slider kit: a single chicken slider (the #4 combo configuration), fries, ranch, themed packaging with Brotherhood of Steel artwork, and the same Stimpak. Heat was your choice of any of the seven spice levels in both.

Is the Dave's Hot Chicken Fallout meal still available?

No. It was a while-supplies-lasted promotion with no restock and no fixed end date, and locations sold out on their own timelines after the January 7, 2026 launch. Pages still describing it as available are launch-era leftovers. The food can be recreated from the regular menu (next answer); the packaging and Stimpak are resale items now.

How do I recreate the Fallout meal now?

Order the parts. For the Bites kit: 10 pc Saucy Bites with fries and a ranch dipper. For the slider kit: combo #4 (one slider with fries) plus ranch. Both are standard menu orders at standard prices. What you cannot recreate is the Fallout box and the Stimpak toy; those were the promotion itself.

What is the Stimpak, and does it actually work?

A replica of Fallout's healing injector, made as a working sauce dispenser: gray with an orange stripe and a meter reading to 300, bagged inside each kit. Fill it with ranch or sauce and the plunger injects it onto the food. It is a functional novelty, not a solid statue, which is why launch reviews kept using it on camera.

Is the Fallout meal spicy?

Only as spicy as you ordered it. The kits were sold at any of Dave's seven spice levels at no extra cost, from Not Hot to Reaper, and the promotion was not a heat challenge. The sauce on the Bites (Buffalo, Spicy BBQ or Hot Honey) is a flavour choice separate from the chicken's heat level.

Was the Fallout meal sold outside the United States?

A UK launch was trailed in the announcement and the Middle East channels teased the collab, with dates varying by market; Canada came with less confirmation. No international dates stand at the standard of the U.S. facts, so none are printed. In every market it was the same while-supplies-lasted format, and it has ended the same way.
